    Reading results honestly¶

    What the tool knows about itself that a reader would otherwise learn the hard way.

    Three things to hold before reading any result:

    • It tells you what the signal did, not whether that is acceptable. A ripple of 25 ns is a measurement; whether it matters depends on what you use the generator for.
    • The generator is the device under test, never the reference. Correctness is arbitrated by injected truth or a declared scenario, not by agreement with the thing being measured.
    • Every detector states when it could not run. A too_short row with a reason is the designed output.

    Reading a detection¶

    A ripple at one cycle per sample of delay traversed may be the instrument, not the generator. That is the shape a correlator manufactures when it cannot place its replica between samples, and this tool did exactly that until 2026-08-20. Its first published finding was retracted for it (Replica placement). It is fixed and held by a test, but keep the reflex: run tools/probes/probe_replica_placement.py, which pins the pointer to the truth so every number it prints should be zero. One minute, no capture needed.

    step fires on a slow ripple, and calls it a step. On one simulator capture: 304 detections across 32 element series against an alpha of 0.01, falling to 8 once the ripple that caused them was found and removed. Those detections were real structure, honestly reported, and misnamed: a difference-of-means statistic responds to a smooth periodic error. If both ripple and step fire on the same series, read the ripple row first. Suppressing steps that a detected ripple explains is a known open item.

    Differencing removes common-mode structure, including structure you want. On a zero-baseline 4-element capture every inter-element difference came back exactly zero, while a large fractional-delay ripple was present in every element. It was common mode, so it cancelled perfectly. Inter-element differences answer element-to-element consistency; they cannot answer whether the generator is right. Look at the single-element series for that.

    A quantum is visible only if the series revisits values. Every uniformly sampled ramp lies exactly on the grid of its own increment, so carrier phase at 1234 Hz Doppler reads as "quantized at 1.234007 cycles". The estimator refuses a grid climbed faster than one step per two samples and says so, but the limit stands: the quantum must be coarser than the measurement noise and finer than the per-epoch motion. Both ends move with C/N0, integration time and Doppler, and neither is a threshold to lower.

    Capture length is a contract¶

    The smallest quantum reliably detected is about 3× the per-epoch code-offset scatter: roughly a quarter sample (21 ns at 12 MHz) at 65 dB-Hz on a 300 ms L1 C/A capture, and nothing at all at 45 dB-Hz. The characteristic-function peak decays as exp(-2π²σ²/q²) while the threshold falls only as sqrt(ln(M/α)/n), so recording for longer barely moves it. C/N0 and coherent integration time are the levers.

    Floor Value Meaning
    Validated 200 ms Where the correlator was measured. Accuracy claims are anchored here.
    Supported 300 ms Where every detector clears its own minimum at a 1 ms code period.

    A capture between them is legal: full pointer, correlation and series, just fewer detectors, and validate names which. Below 200 ms the estimator is unchanged but its accuracy is extrapolated rather than demonstrated, and validate says so.

    Long captures carry a known bias. refine fits one Doppler to the whole series and corrects the code by the series' mean error. For a delay with range acceleration a over a series of length T, that leaves −aT²/12 at the start epoch: up to about 6 cm on a 2 s capture of a real satellite, different per satellite. Correct absolute delays on long captures by it, or keep captures short (where it was found).

    Numbers not to quote unqualified¶

    • C/N0 from a noiseless digital source. With no thermal noise the estimator still returns a number, but what it divides by is inter-satellite cross-correlation and quantization, neither white nor independent. Set source_chain: "digital" and the tool flags it.
    • C/N0 below about 38 dB-Hz on a 1 ms epoch. The carrier fit unwraps a doubled angle so that a navigation data bit cannot masquerade as a half-cycle step, and the symbol is then decided per epoch. Both need the per-epoch SNR to hold up; below that point measurements.cn0_dbhz reads low and the code offset degrades with it. observables.cn0_dbhz is unaffected: it estimates its phase per window and holds to 0.15 dB down to 32 dB-Hz.
    • dop_hz, which is differentiated from phase and noisy by construction. For a Doppler figure, use the correlator's own estimate from measurements.
    • A tracking receiver's code phase, Doppler and carrier phase. These are closed-loop, and the loop smooths away the fractional-delay structure this tool exists to find. They are a pointer and a cross-check, never a measurement. Reaching for them because they are already there is the standard wrong turn.

    • A satellite that acquires but is not in the scenario. On a noiseless source a cross-correlation peak can clear the 38 dB-Hz acquisition threshold, which is tuned for RF. With one hot satellite and no noise, every searched PRN can acquire. The census keeps every PRN searched, so the margin is visible:

      gnss-vsa query store "SELECT sat, cn0_dbhz, acquired FROM acquisitions ORDER BY cn0_dbhz DESC"
